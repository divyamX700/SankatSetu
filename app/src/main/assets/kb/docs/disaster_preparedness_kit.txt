# Disaster Preparedness and Emergency Kit
Source: Adapted from NDMA India household preparedness guidance and IFRC/Red Cross family preparedness guidance — authored summary, with citations per section below

## Building a Basic Emergency Kit
Keep a kit ready with: drinking water (at least 3 liters per person per day for 3 days), non-perishable food that needs no cooking, a flashlight with spare batteries, a battery or hand-crank radio, a first aid kit, any regular medications family members need, copies of important documents (ID, insurance, medical records) in a waterproof bag, cash in small denominations, a whistle to signal for help, and a power bank for phones. Check and refresh food, water, and batteries every few months. [Source: NDMA India household preparedness guidance / IFRC family preparedness guidance]

## Family Emergency Plan
Agree in advance on two meeting points — one near home for a sudden local emergency like a fire, and one further away in case you cannot return home at all. Choose an out-of-area contact person that all family members can call or message if local communication is disrupted, since it is sometimes easier to reach someone far away than someone a few streets over during a local network overload. Make sure every family member, including children, knows the plan and the emergency numbers. [Source: IFRC/Red Cross family preparedness guidance]

## Preparing for Your Specific Local Hazards
Identify the disasters most likely in your area — flooding, earthquake, cyclone, wildfire — and prepare specifically for those. For flood-prone areas, know your evacuation route to higher ground. For earthquake-prone areas, secure heavy furniture and know safe spots in each room. For cyclone-prone coastal areas, know your community's shelter locations and evacuation timing. [Source: NDMA India household preparedness guidance]

## Preparing This App for a Crisis
Keep Sankat Setu installed and Bluetooth permission granted before a crisis happens, not during one. Familiarize yourself with the Chat and Assistant tabs when things are calm, so you know how to use them quickly under stress. Keep your phone charged and consider keeping a small power bank in your emergency kit specifically for this purpose, since the mesh chat and offline assistant both continue working with no cellular network or Wi-Fi at all, as long as your phone has battery and Bluetooth is on. [Source: Sankat Setu app documentation]

## Practicing Your Plan
A plan that has never been practiced often fails under real stress. Periodically walk through your evacuation route, practice the family meeting point plan, and check that kit supplies have not expired or gone stale. Include children in age-appropriate practice so the actions become familiar rather than frightening if a real emergency happens. [Source: IFRC/Red Cross family preparedness guidance]

## Additional Kit Items for Specific Household Needs
Tailor your kit to your household: include extra formula, diapers, and comfort items for infants; spare glasses, hearing aid batteries, or mobility aids for elderly or disabled family members; and a supply of any regular prescription medication that would be hard to replace quickly during a disaster, along with a written list of medications and dosages in case a caregiver is separated from the person who needs them. For pets, keep a few days of food, water, and a leash or carrier ready, since many public shelters restrict or cannot accommodate animals without prior arrangement. [Source: IFRC/Red Cross family preparedness guidance, general household preparedness guidance for vulnerable groups]

## Document and Financial Preparedness
Keep photocopies or photographs (stored offline on your phone as well as on paper) of identity documents, land records, ration cards, insurance policies, and bank account details in a waterproof bag or container that can be grabbed quickly. Losing original documents in a disaster can significantly delay access to relief assistance, compensation, and government schemes afterward, so having copies — even simple phone photos backed up in more than one place — meaningfully speeds up recovery. [Source: NDMA India household preparedness guidance]

## Community-Level Preparedness
Preparedness is not only a household matter — know your community's designated relief shelters, the location of the nearest health facility, and whether your local panchayat or ward has a disaster management committee or early warning system. Participating in any community disaster drills organized locally builds familiarity that individual household planning alone cannot fully replicate, and helps neighbours look out for one another, particularly the elderly, disabled, and those living alone. [Source: NDMA India community-based disaster management guidance]
