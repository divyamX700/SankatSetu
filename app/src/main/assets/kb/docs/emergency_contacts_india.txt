# Emergency Contacts and Helplines in India
Source: Public emergency helpline numbers, Government of India, NDMA India — authored summary; verify locally as numbers can change, with citations per section below

## National Emergency Number
Dial 112 for any emergency in India — police, fire, or medical. This single number, the Emergency Response Support System, connects to police, fire, and ambulance services and is available across most of the country, including via SMS in some states where calling is not possible. [Source: Government of India Emergency Response Support System (ERSS) public information]

## Specific Service Helplines
Police: 100 (also reachable via 112). Fire: 101 (also reachable via 112). Ambulance: 102 or 108 depending on the state. Women's helpline: 1091. Child helpline: 1098. Disaster management helpline (NDMA): 1078. Railway enquiry and accident helpline: 139. [Source: Government of India public emergency helpline directory]

## When You Cannot Get a Call Through
During a major disaster, phone networks are often congested or damaged. If calls will not connect, try SMS to 112 where supported, try again after a short wait as networks recover capacity, or seek help in person from the nearest police station, hospital, or relief camp. This app's Bluetooth mesh chat can also relay a request for help to nearby phones running the app even with no cellular or internet connectivity at all, which can then be passed along until it reaches someone able to get a call through. [Source: Sankat Setu app documentation]

## Information to Have Ready When Calling
Your exact location (address, landmark, or GPS coordinates), the nature of the emergency, number of people affected, and any immediate hazards (fire, gas leak, structural collapse). Stay on the line if it is safe to do so, and follow any instructions given by the dispatcher. [Source: general emergency dispatch guidance]

## State Disaster Management Authorities
Each Indian state has its own State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA) that coordinates local disaster response, evacuation orders, and relief camps. Local guidance from district administration and SDMA should be followed alongside national helplines, since they have the most current, location-specific information during an unfolding event. [Source: NDMA India institutional structure guidance]

## Other Commonly Needed National Helplines
Some other national helplines that can be useful during and after a crisis include the national health helpline for general medical guidance, poison control information available through hospital emergency departments, and senior citizen helplines available in several states. Numbers and availability for these secondary helplines can vary by state, so it is worth noting down the specific ones that apply to your area when you are not in an emergency, alongside the core national numbers, so they are ready if needed. [Source: Government of India public helpline directories — specific secondary helpline numbers vary by state and should be verified locally]

## Using Official Sources for the Most Current Numbers
Helpline numbers occasionally change, and new dedicated lines are sometimes introduced for specific disaster events. Where possible, confirm current numbers through official government websites, local district administration announcements, or radio and television emergency broadcasts, particularly during an active disaster response when temporary helplines specific to that event may be set up. [Source: NDMA India public communication guidance]
