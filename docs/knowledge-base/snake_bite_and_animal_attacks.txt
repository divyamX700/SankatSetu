# Snake Bite and Animal Attacks
Source: Adapted from WHO snakebite envenoming guidance and rabies post-exposure guidance (authored summary)

## Immediate Steps After a Snake Bite
Keep the person as calm and still as possible — movement and panic speed up venom spreading through the body. Remove any rings, watches, or tight clothing near the bite before swelling starts. Keep the bitten limb immobilized and at or slightly below heart level. Get the person to a hospital as quickly as possible; antivenom is the only real treatment for a venomous bite, and time matters.

## What Not to Do for a Snake Bite
Do not cut the wound, do not attempt to suck out venom, do not apply a tight tourniquet, do not apply ice, and do not give the person alcohol or medication without medical advice. These traditional remedies do not work and can cause additional harm, including infection, tissue damage, or restricted blood flow. If possible, try to remember the snake's color and pattern to describe to medical staff, but do not risk further bites trying to catch or kill it.

## Signs of Serious Envenomation
Watch for swelling spreading up the limb, severe pain, difficulty breathing, drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing or speaking, bleeding from the gums or in urine, or the person becoming drowsy or losing consciousness — these indicate the venom is affecting the body seriously and the person needs urgent hospital care.

## Dog Bites and Rabies Risk
Wash any animal bite or scratch immediately and thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes — this alone significantly reduces rabies transmission risk. Apply an antiseptic if available. Do not close the wound with stitches immediately if rabies is a concern, as this can trap the virus; let a medical professional assess it. Seek medical care promptly for a rabies vaccination course, especially from a bite by an unknown, stray, or wild animal, since rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms begin but is preventable with timely vaccination.

## Insect Stings and Allergic Reactions
For a bee or wasp sting, remove the stinger if visible by scraping it out sideways with a fingernail or card rather than squeezing it, which can inject more venom. Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling and pain. Watch closely for signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis): difficulty breathing, swelling of the face, lips or throat, widespread hives, dizziness, or fainting. Anaphylaxis is life-threatening and needs emergency care immediately; if the person has a prescribed epinephrine auto-injector, help them use it without delay.

## Monkey and Wild Animal Encounters
Do not run from or make direct eye contact with an aggressive monkey or wild animal, as this can provoke a chase or attack. Back away slowly, avoid sudden movements, and do not carry visible food. If bitten or scratched, treat it the same as a dog bite: wash thoroughly and seek medical care for possible rabies exposure.
