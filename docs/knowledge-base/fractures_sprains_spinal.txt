# Fractures, Sprains, and Spinal Injury
Source: Adapted from ICRC/Red Cross first aid guidance (authored summary)

## Recognizing a Possible Fracture
Suspect a broken bone if there is severe pain, swelling, visible deformity, inability to move or bear weight on the limb, or a grating sensation. Do not try to straighten a deformed limb or force it back into position. Do not let the person eat or drink in case surgery is needed later.

## Immobilizing a Fracture
Keep the injured limb still and support it in the position it was found, using padding and a splint (a straight, rigid object such as a stick, rolled newspaper, or piece of wood) bound with cloth strips above and below the injury site, not directly over it. For an arm injury, a sling made from a folded cloth or shirt tied around the neck can support the arm. For a suspected leg fracture, you can also bind the injured leg gently to the uninjured leg for support. Check that bindings are not so tight that they cut off circulation — fingers or toes beyond the splint should stay warm and their normal color.

## Suspected Spinal Injury
Suspect a spinal injury after a fall from height, a road accident, a diving accident, or any severe trauma to the head, neck, or back — especially if the person reports numbness, tingling, weakness, or cannot move their limbs. Do not move the person unless there is immediate danger such as fire or floodwater. If you must move them, keep the head, neck, and spine aligned as a single unit with several people helping (a "log roll"), rather than bending or twisting the body. Keep the person still, calm, and warm while waiting for trained emergency responders, who are equipped to move a spinal injury safely.

## Sprains and Strains
For a sprained ankle, wrist, or joint without signs of a full fracture, follow the R.I.C.E. approach: Rest the joint and avoid putting weight on it; Ice the area with a cloth-wrapped cold pack for 15-20 minutes at a time to reduce swelling; Compress gently with an elastic bandage, not so tight it restricts blood flow; Elevate the limb above heart level when possible. If pain is severe, the joint looks deformed, or the person cannot bear any weight at all, treat it as a possible fracture instead and seek medical care.

## Head Injury Warning Signs
After any head injury, watch for worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion or unusual drowsiness, one pupil larger than the other, seizures, clear fluid coming from the nose or ears, or loss of consciousness even briefly — any of these needs urgent medical evaluation. Keep the person still, monitor their breathing and responsiveness, and do not give them food, drink, or pain medication until they have been checked by a medical professional.
