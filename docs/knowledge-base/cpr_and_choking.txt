# CPR, Cardiac Arrest, and Choking
Source: Adapted from ICRC/Red Cross first aid guidance and WHO basic life support principles (authored summary)

## Recognizing Cardiac Arrest
If a person is unresponsive — not moving, not reacting to being shaken or shouted at — and not breathing normally (no breathing, or only occasional gasping), treat this as cardiac arrest. Call for emergency help or ask someone nearby to call 112 immediately, and start CPR without delay. Every minute without CPR or defibrillation reduces the chance of survival significantly.

## Performing Chest Compressions
Kneel beside the person on a firm, flat surface. Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest, between the nipples, and your other hand on top with fingers interlocked. Keep your arms straight and push hard and fast, at least 5 centimeters deep, at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute — roughly the tempo of the song "Stayin' Alive." Allow the chest to fully rise between compressions. If you are trained in rescue breaths, give 30 compressions followed by 2 breaths; if untrained or unwilling to give breaths, continuous hands-only chest compressions are still highly effective and better than doing nothing.

## Continuing CPR
Do not stop CPR until emergency medical help arrives and takes over, an automated defibrillator becomes available and is used, the person starts breathing normally and moving on their own, or you are physically too exhausted to continue safely. If multiple people are present, switch who is doing compressions every 2 minutes to avoid fatigue reducing compression quality.

## Choking in Adults and Children Over One Year
Ask the person "Are you choking?" If they can cough, speak, or breathe, encourage them to keep coughing forcefully — do not intervene. If they cannot cough, speak, or breathe, stand behind them, lean them forward, and give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. If this does not clear the blockage, perform abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich maneuver): stand behind the person, wrap your arms around their waist, make a fist just above their navel, grasp it with your other hand, and pull sharply inward and upward. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object is expelled or the person becomes unresponsive.

## Choking in Infants Under One Year
Never perform abdominal thrusts on an infant. Lay the infant face-down along your forearm, supporting the head and jaw, with the head lower than the chest. Give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. If unsuccessful, turn the infant face-up along your other arm and give up to 5 chest thrusts using two fingers on the center of the chest, just below the nipple line. Alternate until the object clears or the infant becomes unresponsive, in which case begin infant CPR and get emergency help.

## If the Person Becomes Unresponsive While Choking
Lower them carefully to the ground, call for emergency help immediately, and begin CPR. Each time you open the airway to give a rescue breath, look inside the mouth for the object and remove it only if you can clearly see and reach it — do not do a blind finger sweep, as this can push the object deeper.
