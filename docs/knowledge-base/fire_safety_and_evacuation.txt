# Fire Safety and Evacuation
Source: Adapted from NFPA public fire safety guidance and general building evacuation principles (authored summary)

## If a Fire Starts
Alert everyone nearby immediately by shouting "fire" and, if there is a fire alarm, activate it. Get out of the building using the nearest safe exit — do not stop to collect belongings. If you are trained and the fire is small and contained (like a wastebasket fire), you may attempt to use a fire extinguisher; otherwise, prioritize evacuating over fighting the fire.

## Moving Through Smoke
Smoke and toxic gases are the leading cause of fire deaths, more than burns. If a room is filling with smoke, stay low — crawl on the floor where the air is clearer and cooler, since smoke and heat rise. Cover your nose and mouth with a damp cloth if possible to filter some smoke.

## Checking Doors Before Opening
Before opening any closed door while escaping a fire, feel the door and door handle with the back of your hand. If it is hot, do not open it — fire may be on the other side; find another way out or seal gaps with cloth and signal for help from a window instead. If the door is cool, open it slowly and be ready to close it quickly if smoke or heat rushes in.

## If Your Clothes Catch Fire
Stop moving immediately — do not run, as this fans the flames. Drop to the ground and roll back and forth to smother the fire. Cover your face with your hands while rolling. Once flames are out, cool any burns under running water for 20 minutes.

## If You Are Trapped
If you cannot escape, go to a room with a window, close the door between you and the fire, and seal gaps under the door with wet towels or clothing to slow smoke entry. Open the window if it is safe to do so for fresh air, and signal for help by shouting or waving a bright cloth. Call 112 or your local fire emergency number and tell them your exact location if you can.

## Home Fire Prevention and Escape Planning
Know at least two ways out of every room in your home in advance, and agree on an outside meeting point with your family. Keep exits clear of clutter. Never leave cooking fires, candles, or heaters unattended. Keep flammable materials away from stoves and open flames. If you use a gas cylinder, check for leaks regularly (a soapy water test on connections works well) and keep the area ventilated.
