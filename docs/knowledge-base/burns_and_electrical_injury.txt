# Burns and Electrical Injury
Source: Adapted from WHO burn care guidance and ICRC first aid principles (authored summary)

## Immediate Cooling of a Burn
Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes as soon as possible — this is the single most effective first aid step and reduces pain and depth of injury even if started up to 3 hours after the burn. Remove any jewellery, tight clothing, or watches near the burn before swelling starts, unless they are stuck to the skin. Do not use ice, iced water, butter, oil, toothpaste, or ointments on a fresh burn.

## Covering a Burn
After cooling, cover the burn loosely with a clean, non-fluffy material such as plastic cling film (placed loosely, not wrapped tightly) or a clean cloth. Do not burst any blisters that form. Do not apply any home remedies, ash, or cow dung to a burn, as these significantly increase infection risk.

## When a Burn Needs Hospital Care
Seek medical care immediately if the burn is larger than the person's palm, is on the face, hands, feet, genitals, or a major joint, looks white, brown, or charred (deep burn), is caused by chemicals or electricity, or if the person is a young child, elderly, or has difficulty breathing. Burns around the mouth, nose, or from a fire in an enclosed space can mean airway injury from smoke or heat — this is a medical emergency even without visible burns.

## Electrical Injury Safety
Before touching someone who has had an electric shock, make sure the power source is switched off or the person is no longer in contact with it. If you cannot turn off the power, use a dry, non-conductive object like a wooden stick or dry rope to move the person or the wire away — never use your bare hands or a metal object. Once safe to approach, check if the person is breathing and responsive; electrical injury can cause cardiac arrest, so be ready to start CPR. Electrical burns often look small on the skin surface but can cause severe internal damage, so anyone who has had a significant electric shock needs medical evaluation even if they seem fine.

## Chemical Burns
For a chemical burn, brush off any dry chemical powder first, then flush the area with large amounts of clean running water for at least 20 minutes, removing contaminated clothing while flushing. Avoid getting the chemical on yourself. Seek medical help, and if possible identify the chemical involved to tell responders.
