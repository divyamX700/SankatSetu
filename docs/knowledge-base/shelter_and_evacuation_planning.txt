# Shelter and Evacuation Planning
Source: Adapted from NDMA India and Sphere humanitarian shelter standards (authored summary)

## Deciding to Evacuate
If authorities issue an evacuation order, follow it — these are based on hazard forecasts and past disaster patterns that are difficult to judge from personal experience alone. If no official order exists but you observe clear danger signs (rapidly rising water, structural cracks, a wildfire approaching, a strong gas smell), do not wait for permission to leave; evacuate early rather than at the last minute when routes may be blocked or more dangerous.

## What to Bring When Evacuating
Prioritize, if time allows: identification documents and cash, any essential medication, water and some food, a phone and power bank, a flashlight, and weather-appropriate clothing. Keep bags packed in advance if you live in a hazard-prone area so you can leave quickly without searching for items under stress.

## Choosing an Evacuation Route
Use routes designated by local authorities where possible, since they account for known hazards. Avoid low-lying roads, riverbanks, and bridges during flood risk, and avoid downed power lines and unstable structures during earthquakes or storms. If your primary route is blocked, have a backup route in mind rather than improvising in the moment.

## At a Relief Camp or Shelter
Register your name and the names of everyone with you at the camp entry point, both for your own safety record and to help family members searching for you. Follow camp rules on space allocation, food distribution timing, and sanitation facility use — these exist to keep a crowded, stressed environment functioning safely for everyone. Report any urgent medical needs to camp staff immediately, and report any safety concerns (including harassment or unsafe conditions) to camp management or authorities.

## Sanitation and Health in a Shelter Setting
Wash hands with soap before eating and after using toilets, since crowded shelter conditions increase risk of disease spread. Keep children close and supervised, especially near cooking areas and any open water sources. Report symptoms of illness in yourself or others to camp health workers early, both for your own care and to prevent spread to others in a crowded setting.

## Returning Home After Evacuation
Do not return home until authorities confirm it is safe — a hazard like flooding or a damaged structure may not be visible or may still be actively dangerous even after the main event has passed. When you do return, inspect for structural damage, gas leaks, and electrical hazards before fully re-entering and resuming normal use of utilities.
