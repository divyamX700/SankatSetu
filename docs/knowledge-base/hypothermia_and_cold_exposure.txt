# Hypothermia and Cold Exposure
Source: Adapted from WHO and Red Cross cold-weather safety guidance (authored summary)

## Recognizing Hypothermia
Hypothermia is when body temperature drops dangerously low, most often from cold weather, wet clothing, or cold water immersion. Early signs include shivering, tiredness, confusion, and clumsiness. As it worsens, shivering may actually stop (a dangerous sign, not a good one), and the person may become very confused, slur speech, move slowly, or lose consciousness. Severe hypothermia is life-threatening and needs emergency care.

## Treating Hypothermia
Move the person to a warm, dry place and out of the wind as soon as possible. Remove any wet clothing and replace with dry, warm layers or blankets, covering the head as well since significant heat is lost there. Warm the person gradually with body heat (lying close together), warm (not hot) dry compresses to the neck, chest, and groin, and warm sweet drinks if they are fully alert and able to swallow — never give alcohol, which worsens heat loss. Handle a severely hypothermic person gently, as rough movement can trigger a dangerous heart rhythm; get them to emergency medical care as soon as possible.

## Frostbite
Frostbite is localized freezing of skin and tissue, most common on fingers, toes, ears, and nose. Affected skin may look white, greyish, or waxy and feel numb or hard. Do not rub or massage frostbitten skin, and do not use direct high heat like a fire or heater, as numb tissue can burn without the person feeling it. Instead, warm the area gradually in warm (not hot) water around body temperature, and seek medical care, especially if numbness persists or blisters form.

## Preventing Cold Exposure Emergencies
Dress in layers, keeping an outer layer that blocks wind and moisture. Keep hands, feet, ears, and head well covered, since these lose heat fastest. Stay dry — wet clothing conducts heat away from the body far faster than dry clothing. If travelling in cold conditions, tell someone your route and expected return time, and carry emergency supplies including extra dry clothing, food, and a way to signal for help.
