# Missing Person and Family Reunification
Source: Adapted from ICRC restoring family links principles and general disaster reunification guidance (authored summary)

## Immediately After Getting Separated
If your family or group gets separated during a disaster or evacuation, agree in advance (or as soon as reunited briefly) on a specific meeting point outside the danger area, and a backup meeting point further away in case the first is not accessible. If phones are working, call or message immediately; if not, use this app's Bluetooth mesh chat, which can relay messages between nearby phones without any cellular network — announce your name, description, and location so nearby users running the app can pass the message along.

## Reporting a Missing Person to Authorities
Report a missing person to local police, disaster relief authorities, or a designated relief camp registration desk as soon as possible. Provide a recent photo if available, full name, age, physical description, clothing last seen wearing, any medical conditions or medication needs, and the location and time they were last seen. Register at official relief camps and shelters even briefly, since these lists are often used to help reunite families.

## Searching for a Missing Family Member
Check official relief camps, hospitals, and shelters in the area methodically, since injured or displaced people are often taken to the nearest available facility rather than one you'd expect. Many disaster responses set up a central missing persons registry or family tracing service — ask local authorities or Red Cross/Red Crescent volunteers if one has been established for this event, as these track separated family reports across multiple camps and hospitals.

## Helping a Found Child or Lost Person
If you find a child or a confused person who appears lost or separated from their family, do not leave them alone. Keep them calm, ask their name and any information about their family if they are able to communicate, and take them to the nearest relief camp registration point, police, or officially marked help desk rather than trying to search for the family yourself, which can cause you to miss each other. Note the exact time and location where you found them, which helps authorities match them with a family search report.

## Staying Reachable
If you are safe but away from home, let family and neighbours know through any means available — a note left at your home, a message through this app's mesh chat to be relayed by others, or word passed through a relief camp — so people searching for you are not left worrying or wasting search effort.
