# Earthquake Safety
Source: Adapted from NDMA India earthquake guidelines and USGS/FEMA public safety guidance (authored summary)

## During Shaking: Drop, Cover, Hold On
The moment you feel shaking, drop to your hands and knees where you are, take cover under a sturdy table or desk if one is nearby, and hold on to it until the shaking stops. If no table is available, cover your head and neck with your arms and crouch against an interior wall, away from windows, mirrors, and heavy furniture that could fall. Do not run outside during shaking — most earthquake injuries happen from falling objects and people falling while trying to move, not from buildings collapsing on people who stayed put.

## If You Are Outdoors
Move to an open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and power lines, since these can collapse or fall. Drop to the ground and stay there until the shaking stops.

## If You Are in a Vehicle
Pull over to a clear location away from buildings, trees, overpasses, and power lines, stop the vehicle, and stay inside with your seatbelt on until the shaking stops. Avoid bridges or ramps that may have been damaged.

## Immediately After Shaking Stops
Expect aftershocks — they can be as strong as the initial quake in the following minutes, hours, or days. Check yourself and people near you for injuries and provide first aid before moving anyone with a suspected serious injury. If you are in a damaged building, evacuate carefully using the stairs, not elevators, watching for broken glass, downed wires, and unstable structures. If you smell gas or hear a hissing sound, leave immediately and get far away, and avoid using matches, lighters, or electrical switches which could ignite a leak.

## After the Earthquake
Once outside and safe, do not re-enter damaged buildings until authorities confirm they are structurally safe. Stay away from damaged areas, coastlines if a tsunami warning is possible, and areas with visible structural damage. Use text messages or the mesh chat rather than voice calls where possible, since phone networks are often overloaded after a disaster — this app's Bluetooth mesh chat continues to work without any cellular or Wi-Fi network at all. Listen to official radio or emergency broadcasts for guidance, and check on neighbours, especially elderly or disabled people, if it is safe to do so.

## Preparing Before an Earthquake
Identify safe spots in each room (under sturdy furniture, against interior walls) in advance. Secure heavy furniture, shelves, and water heaters to walls where possible. Keep a flashlight, first aid kit, drinking water, and a battery or hand-crank radio easily accessible. Know how to shut off gas, water, and electricity at the main switches.
