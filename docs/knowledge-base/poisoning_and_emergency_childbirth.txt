# Poisoning and Emergency Childbirth
Source: Adapted from WHO poison control guidance and basic emergency obstetric first aid principles (authored summary)

## Suspected Poisoning — General Steps
If you suspect someone has been poisoned (swallowed, inhaled, or absorbed through skin), first ensure your own safety before approaching, especially with gas or chemical exposure. Try to identify what was involved — keep the container, plant, or substance if possible to show medical staff. Call emergency services or a poison control helpline immediately and follow their specific guidance, since treatment differs greatly depending on the substance.

## What Not to Do for Poisoning
Do not make the person vomit unless specifically told to by a medical professional or poison control — for some substances (like strong acids, alkalis, or petroleum products), vomiting can cause additional serious injury on the way back up. Do not give the person food, water, milk, or any home remedy without medical guidance, since this can also be harmful depending on the poison.

## If the Person Is Unconscious from Poisoning
Check breathing and responsiveness. If they are breathing but unconscious, place them in the recovery position (on their side, head tilted back slightly, to keep the airway clear and prevent choking on vomit) and monitor closely until help arrives. If they are not breathing, begin CPR. Get emergency help immediately in either case.

## Emergency Childbirth — When Birth Cannot Wait
If a birth is happening and there is no time to reach a hospital, stay calm and call for medical help or guidance by phone if any connection is available. Have the mother lie down in a comfortable position, ideally on a clean surface with clean cloths nearby. Let the birth happen naturally — do not pull on the baby. Support the baby's head and body gently as it emerges; babies are slippery, so hold securely.

## Immediately After the Baby is Born
Dry the baby gently with a clean cloth and keep them warm, skin-to-skin with the mother if possible, covered with a blanket. Most babies cry and start breathing within moments; if a baby does not seem to be breathing, gently rub their back and the soles of their feet to stimulate them. Do not cut the umbilical cord unless you have clean, sterile equipment and know how to do so safely — it is generally safe to leave it attached and wait for medical help, keeping the placenta at the same level as the baby if it has not yet delivered.

## Caring for Mother After Birth
The placenta will usually deliver within 30 minutes after the baby; do not pull on the umbilical cord to hurry this. Watch for heavy bleeding after birth (soaking through cloth rapidly) — this needs urgent medical attention. Keep the mother warm, encourage her to rest, and get both mother and baby to a medical facility for proper checkup as soon as possible, even if the birth seemed to go smoothly.
