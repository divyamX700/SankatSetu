# Flood Safety
Source: Adapted from NDMA India flood guidelines and FEMA flood safety guidance (authored summary)

## Before Flooding Arrives
If authorities issue a flood warning for your area, move valuables, important documents, and food supplies to higher levels of your home. Charge your phone and power banks fully, fill clean containers with drinking water, and keep this app's mesh chat ready — it works without cellular network or Wi-Fi. Know your area's evacuation routes and the nearest official shelter location in advance.

## Never Walk or Drive Through Moving Water
Just 15 centimeters (6 inches) of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet, and 30 centimeters (1 foot) can float and sweep away most cars. Do not attempt to walk, swim, or drive through flood water, even if it looks shallow or slow — the current strength and depth are hard to judge, and the road surface underneath may already be washed away. "Turn around, don't drown" is the standard safety rule for a reason: it is the leading cause of flood deaths.

## During a Flood
Move to higher ground immediately if flooding is rising near you — do not wait for official evacuation orders if your area is clearly at risk. Avoid contact with flood water where possible; it is often contaminated with sewage, chemicals, or debris and can carry waterborne diseases. If trapped in a building, move to the highest level, but avoid closed attics where you could become trapped by rising water — go to the roof if necessary and signal for help.

## Electrical Safety in Flooded Areas
Do not touch electrical equipment, switches, or wires if you are wet or standing in water. Turn off electricity at the main breaker if you can do so safely without standing in water. Assume all downed power lines are live and stay well away from them, and report them to authorities if you have a way to.

## After Flood Waters Recede
Avoid returning home until authorities declare it safe — floodwater can weaken building foundations, roads, and bridges in ways that are not visible. Do not drink tap water until it has been declared safe or you have purified it yourself (see the water purification guidance). Discard any food that came into contact with flood water. Watch for snakes and other displaced animals that may be sheltering in debris or flooded structures. Wear boots and gloves when cleaning up to avoid contaminated water and sharp debris.

## Health Risks After Flooding
Standing water breeds mosquitoes, increasing risk of dengue and malaria in the following weeks — remove standing water where you can and use mosquito nets. Watch for symptoms of waterborne illness like diarrhea, vomiting, or fever in yourself and family members, and seek medical care if these occur, especially in young children and the elderly.
