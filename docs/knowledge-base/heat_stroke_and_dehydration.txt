# Heat Stroke, Heat Exhaustion, and Dehydration
Source: Adapted from WHO and NDMA India heat wave advisories (authored summary)

## Heat Exhaustion vs Heat Stroke
Heat exhaustion causes heavy sweating, cold or clammy skin, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, and muscle cramps, with body temperature usually normal or only mildly raised. Heat stroke is a medical emergency: body temperature rises above 40°C (104°F), skin becomes hot and dry or occasionally still sweaty, and the person may become confused, slur speech, have a rapid pulse, seize, or lose consciousness. Heat stroke can be fatal within a short time and needs emergency medical help immediately.

## Treating Heat Exhaustion
Move the person to a cool or shaded place immediately. Have them lie down and raise their legs slightly. Loosen or remove excess clothing. Cool their skin with a damp cloth, fan them, and give small, frequent sips of cool water or an oral rehydration solution if they are alert and able to swallow. They should feel better within 30 minutes; if not, or symptoms worsen, treat as heat stroke and seek emergency care.

## Treating Heat Stroke
Call for emergency medical help immediately — this is life-threatening. While waiting, move the person to shade or a cool area, remove excess clothing, and cool them as rapidly as possible: apply cool wet cloths or water to the skin, especially the neck, armpits, and groin where large blood vessels are close to the surface, and fan them continuously. If ice or cold packs are available, apply them to the same areas. Do not give an unconscious or confused person anything to drink, due to choking risk.

## Preventing Heat-Related Illness
During extreme heat, drink water regularly throughout the day even before feeling thirsty, and avoid excess tea, coffee, and alcohol which increase fluid loss. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity during the hottest part of the day, typically noon to 4pm. Wear light-colored, loose, breathable clothing and a hat or cloth covering when outdoors. Check on elderly neighbours, young children, and anyone with chronic illness during heat waves, as they are at higher risk.

## Oral Rehydration for Dehydration
For dehydration from heat, diarrhea, or vomiting, an oral rehydration solution helps the body absorb fluid faster than water alone. If a packaged ORS sachet is not available, mix 6 level teaspoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of salt into 1 liter of clean or boiled-and-cooled water. Give small, frequent sips rather than large amounts at once, especially to children, to avoid vomiting.
